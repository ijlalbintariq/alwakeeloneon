In the criminal justice system of Pakistan, the grant of post-arrest bail is governed by the principle that "liberty of a person is the rule and jail is the exception." ### Legal Context
Post-arrest bail under the **Code of Criminal Procedure** is categorized into two main types: cases falling within the prohibitory clause (offences punishable by death, life imprisonment, or 10 years) and those that do not. However, regardless of the severity of the offence, if the court finds that there are no reasonable grounds for believing the accused has committed a non-bailable offence, but there are sufficient grounds for further inquiry into his guilt, the accused becomes entitled to bail as a matter of right under the concept of "further inquiry."

### Statutory Framework
The primary provision governing this relief is found in the **Code of Criminal Procedure**.

* **Section 497(2) of the Code of Criminal Procedure**: This section mandates that if it appears to the court at any stage of the investigation, inquiry, or trial that there are no reasonable grounds for believing the accused has committed a non-bailable offence, but there are sufficient grounds for further inquiry into his guilt, the accused shall be released on bail.
* **Section 22 of the Code of Criminal Procedure**: Relates to the local jurisdiction of Sessions Judges and Magistrates, ensuring that the bail application is moved before the court within whose local limits the offence was committed.
